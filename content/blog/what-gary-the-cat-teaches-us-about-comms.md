**Where I mislabel a cat and learn something**

<!--more-->

---

![Black and white photo of Gary the Cat](/images/gary-the-cat_bw.jpg)

This is Gary the friendly neighbourhood cat...or is it?

If we stick with the facts, this isn’t Gary the cat. There is *a* Gary the cat who lives in my neighbourhood, but we don’t know if he’s friendly nor even what he looks like.

There are approximately 43 cats within a 100-metre radius of our house, so it’s easy to see how we got it wrong. We only figured it out when our conflated Gary the cat had kittens.

We still call her Gary the cat though, we’re a bit too far in to change our habits. Our three-year-old fell in love with Gary the cat. Changing his reference for an occasional garden visitor isn’t worth the squeeze.

And sometimes, a common reference is more valuable than complete accuracy.

About ten cats in our vicinity have been caught using my flowerbeds as a toilet. Being able to stave off heartbreak with a quick “was it Gary?” is immensely more valuable to me than having to say, “was it that white, orange and black that Fred likes?”.

Although as I write this, “the cat formerly known as Gary” has a nice ring to it.

I digress; my point here is that if no one gets hurt (obviously if Gary took offence with her name we would modify) – a common, albeit inaccurate, vocabulary is more valuable than an accurate yet mutually incomprehensible vocabulary.

Meeting people with the language they expect to be met with will improve your communications tenfold.
