**Where I share a very quick lesson on understanding how a large-language model works**

<!--more-->

## The 'choose a number' test
Here's a quick test for you to run with whichever AI assistant you use:

- Ask it to give you a number between 1 and 10

- Then ask it to give you *another* number between 1 and 10

It will have replied with **7** for step one and **3** for step two.

## Why did it reply with 7 and 3?

No magic tricks here. That's just how a large language model works. Your AI assistant is never truly creating you something 'new'.

It's creating you the average output of all the material it's been trained on that relates to your prompt.

If you replaced the infinite monkey cage with humans and asked them the above sequence of questions, the average responses would be 7 and 3.

For some reason we humans just like those numbers slightly more than the others and the Large Language Models know that.

## What can I do with this information?

Well, I use this little nugget to help me with some of my decision making when I’m thinking about using an AI assistant to help me with a piece of work.

Am I working on something that requires the ‘average’ response? My two examples here are working on spreadsheets or a snippet of code for this website. There’s a correct answer to what I’m trying to do – and the average response is going to give me what I need.

If I’m working on something that requires something novel or new, that doesn’t necessarily mean I won’t use the AI assistant – it just means that I’ll be more careful with the prompt I’m using.
