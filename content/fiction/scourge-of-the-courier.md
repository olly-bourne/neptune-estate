Despite his spectre looming so large on the collective consciousness of biological life across the known universe, relatively little is known about the early years of Stanley the Terrible - Saviour of Sapientkind and Prime Being for Life.

This dearth of information stems from three extenuating factors:
- His early life appears to have been entirely unremarkable
- His hatred appears to have given him an extraordinarily long life, and finding a scholar with the requisite attention span is now nigh on impossible
- His surviving cult considers any scholarly examination of his life an act of extreme apostasy.

On the latter point, the text shared below is all that remains of Humbolt Ehrmantraut’s translation of Morchard the Learned’s *Stanlexicon: The early life of the most terrible.*

The executions, book burnings and subsequent memory damnings that befell both Ehrmantraut and Morchard were justified with the usual cultist line: “He was terrible. He saved us all. Never again. Let’s not go turning over any stones.”

Indeed, some accuse the scholarship of total fabrication. How could a being so terrible yet so blessed begin his life as a mere delivery drone? The fact that the bloodletting paused but once so he could enact sweeping reforms to the Intergalactic Postal Service is surely just a coincidence.

That such banality could lead to the suffering and salvation of so many still haunts those of us who live in the shadow of Stanley.

## Chapter 116 – Sol arrival

The hiss of the landing gear broke the silence in the cockpit, resuming after the dull thud of the ship touched down in the bay.

It was Stanley's first delivery to a dyson sphere, and he was slightly disappointed. It supposedly housed an entire solar system. So why was it so…empty?

Another hiss rang through the cockpit as the ship released the door.

Stanley sat there for five minutes, taking in the silence. 
The ship's computer eventually let out a raspy, "We have arrived at your destination… Sol dyson sphere".

The tardiness of the announcement angered Stanley. He could have been out of the door by now. Another grievance to add to the list.

The silence stayed Stanley's legs. It wasn't peaceful. More…uncanny.

Another moment passed before he got up from his chair and stretched his limbs. How long had he been sitting down now? Fifty years?

By no means the longest delivery he'd ever done, but still long enough to be considered tedious.

He went to the back of the ship and keyed in the destination code. A locker to his right swung open, revealing a medium-sized parcel wrapped in brown paper.

He walked out the door and down the landing ramp. Still no signs of life.

"There had better be a map," he thought.

Sure enough, one hung just outside the hangar door. The parcel was addressed to ‘Viewing Deck', which Stanley quickly identified on the floor plan.

"Well, this should be an easy one!" he thought as he set off with a spring in his step.

## Chapter 117 – Viewing deck

Twenty-six hours later, Stanley stood outside a door with ‘Viewing Deck' glowing in green letters above.

Stanley's body hadn’t minded too much; it was, after all, genetically modified to make a delivery at whatever physical cost. His brain, however, had been nudged ever so closer to oblivion by the extended excursion.

"Whoever designed that map had no sense of scale", Stanley muttered as he stood there, shaking away visions of a cartographer’s feet exposed over hot coals.

Stanley hadn't seen a single soul during the entire walk. While he was physically incapable of feeling lonely, he could feel boredom, and interactions with other beings usually helped to keep that at bay.

'Tap, tap, tap...tap.'

He wasn't sure why he'd knocked. Based on what he'd seen, it was incredibly likely he would have to leave the parcel for the recipient to pick up at some later point.

He was shocked when someone answered.

## Chapter 118 - Deliverance

"Alright, mate, that for me, yeah?" said the being standing on the other side of the threshold.

Stanley wasn't quite sure what to take in first. The majestic panorama of a dying star through the viewing port, or the far less majestic being extending its top two limbs out towards the parcel?

Opting for professionalism, Stanley focused on the parcel's recipient. A humanoid, he noted, and quite a pink one at that. He tapped the side of his head to engage his translator and replied, "I think so, sir; it's addressed to 'Viewing Deck'."

"Yeah, that'll be me." the humanoid replied, "just me here at the moment."

As soon as Stanley handed over the parcel, the box was unwrapped and unsealed, revealing a green can with intricate markings.

Stanley noted the hunger in the being's eyes as it removed the can and flicked a tab at one end. 

A quick 'pssssst' was followed by a rapid 'glug glug glug.'

By the time Stanley stole another glimpse at the dying star in the background, the green can was on the floor; the being had released some gas out of its breathing hole, said "thank you", and slammed the doors back shut.

"Another happy customer..." thought Stanley as he started the silent return to his ship.
